# THE INHERITANCE — Story Bible & Build Plan (v3)
*NASA Space Apps 2026 · Space Mission Design Game · Single-player story mode*

---

## 1. Premise

**Logline:** In an alternate 1960s, the NASA engineer designing humanity's first crewed Moon landing dies before it flies, with things unresolved between him and his kid. The program is shelved. The kid joins NASA at the bottom and climbs one mission at a time, and along the way finds out who their father really was.

**Setting:** A fictional NASA site, the **NASA Lunar Systems Center**, 1961–1969, with real places (Cape Canaveral launches, Houston-style mission control) as backdrops. In this timeline, Dad's Moon program was shelved in 1961. Nobody has tried since.

**Legal/safety rules:** No NASA logos (meatball or worm). All characters are fictional, with no real astronauts or officials. Not presented as an official NASA product. Check the Space Apps rules and FAQ.

**Tone:** LEGO-movie energy. Blocky toy-brick-style people, slapstick, deadpan side characters, and real heart. Funny scenes have something underneath; sad scenes stay a little warm.

**Art direction:** Our own chunky toy-brick-style characters (no LEGO name, logo or exact minifig). 60s palette: mustard, teal, burnt orange, cream. Consoles, reel-to-reel tapes, slide rules, typewriters, CRTs.

**Core theme:** You can't finish someone else's dream. You can only build your own, and let theirs be part of it.

**Emotional arc (protagonist):**
Guilt + anger ("he chose the Moon over me") → curiosity ("who was he at work?") → understanding (the tapes) → letting go (building *their own* rocket, not Dad's).

## 3. Storyline

### ACT I: The Empty Office
- **Cold open (1961):** A CRT news report: Walter, lead designer of NASA's Moon program, has died, and NASA has suspended the program indefinitely.
- **Flashback (the fight):** One week earlier. The protagonist waits at a school science fair next to a homemade rocket. Dad's chair is empty. That night: "You care more about that Moon than me." Dad: "That's not— I'll explain, I promise." He never does.
- **Funeral:** Rain. A hair piece blows off during the eulogy. Jo gives the protagonist Dad's mission patch. Mom stands apart, not looking at anyone from NASA.
- **Joining NASA:** Mom: "Not you too." The protagonist goes anyway, as a filing clerk. Pike: "Program's dead. Go file something."
- **Dad's office:** Late night, the protagonist sneaks in. Dust, a Moon poster, and a broken model rocket on the desk. They find a reel-to-reel tape labelled **"#1"** and play it. **Dad's voice.**

### ACT II: Climbing (Levels 1–5)
Each level = a promotion, a new tape, and more of Dad's office unlocked. Between levels there are quiet home and office scenes.

| # | Year | Mission | Rank | Emotional beat |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1962 | **BEACON-1** first satellite | Clerk (filling in) | First win. Calls home; Mom doesn't pick up. |
| 2 | 1963 | **BISCUIT-1** animal flight & return | Junior engineer | Biscuit comes home → becomes the protagonist's dog. Dot: "Your dad cried at the first one too." |
| 3 | 1964 | **SOLO** first human orbit (Jo) | Systems engineer | First time someone's life depends on them. Jo: "I trust your numbers, kid." |
| 4 | 1966 | **TANDEM** docking + spacewalk | Deputy flight director | Victor nearly fails; they bond over losing parents. |
| 5 | 1967 | **SURVEYOR-L** robotic Moon landing | Flight director | Burnout scene: protagonist sleeping at the office, missing Mom's birthday. They've become Dad. |

**Running mystery:** The tapes reveal SELENE's test failed because the board forced a cheaper heat shield over Dad's objections, and Pike signed off. Dad spent his last year trying to fix it, which is why he was never home.

**Mid-game turn (after L5):** Mom finds the protagonist asleep at Dad's desk. Their first real talk. Mom: "He did that too. Every night. I used to bring him dinner." She brings dinner.

### ACT III: The Plan (Level 6)
- **The discovery:** In Dad's safe, the protagonist finds the *start* of a mission plan. It's not a full blueprint, just early sketches, rough mass numbers, a few crossed-out ideas, and a title page: **"PROJECT [PROTAGONIST'S NAME]."** Dad named the Moon mission after them. The thing they thought he chose over them was *for* them.
- **Pike's confession:** He admits the heat shield decision and approves the program, under its new name.
- **Level 6: Project [NAME], crewed Moon landing (1969).** The player designs the mission *starting from Dad's partial sketches*. It's unfinished, so the player has to complete it with everything they've learned. They can keep Dad's ideas, change them, or throw them out.
- **Final tape (plays on success):** The tape Dad recorded the night of the fight and never gave them.
- **Finale:** Jo steps onto the Moon and leaves Dad's patch. Mom is in mission control, holding the protagonist's hand. The model rocket on Dad's desk is fully fixed.

## 5. Dad's Tapes
*Reel-to-reel, recorded late at night in his office. Half engineering principle, half Dad. They give principles, never answers.*

1. **(Act I)** "Respect the number: how much a rocket can lift. And eat breakfast."
2. **(after L1)** "Coming back is harder than going up. Don't save weight on what keeps someone alive."
3. **(after L2)** "Margins. Leave more than you think you need. I never do. Ruth says that's my whole problem."
4. **(after L3)** "Radios only talk when they can see each other. Plan for silence. ...I'm bad at that at home too."
5. **(after L4)** "Every extra kilo needs fuel to carry the fuel. Rockets are a joke about weight that never ends."
6. **(hidden, the safe)** "Told Pike the cheap shield would fail. It did. I'm not angry. Everyone's scared of the price tag. I'm scared of a lot more than that."
7. **(finale)** The unsent tape from the night of the fight (see CS-10).

## 6. Gameplay: Free Design + Story Triggers

**Design rule:** The story sets the *goal and the limits*, never the design. A systems sim decides every outcome from the player's own build. Many different builds can succeed.

### 6.1 Center Campus (hub)
| Area | What you do there |
|---|---|
| **Pike's Office** | Mission brief (goal, budget, deadline) |
| **Design Bay (Dot)** | Structure, power, batteries, computer, attitude control, shielding |
| **Science Lab (Fenwick)** | Instruments, optional science goals |
| **Comms Room (Sparks)** | Radio, ground stations, tape recorder vs live downlink |
| **Launch Office** | Rocket + orbit; mass/orbit compatibility check |
| **Sim Room** | Run the simulation as many times as you like |
| **Dad's Office** | Tapes, the model rocket, story progress |

Areas can be visited in any order, and anything can be changed before launch.

### 6.2 Parts catalog
Each part has: Mass · Power use/gen · Cost · Data rate · Reliability · Level unlocked.

### 6.3 The sim (no scripted outcomes)
- **Launch:** mass ≤ rocket capacity for the chosen orbit, or launch is blocked
- **Power:** generation + batteries cover the load through eclipses
- **Data:** data generated fits storage + comms windows
- **Lifetime:** set by orbit height + consumables
- **Re-entry / landing:** shield and fuel vs heat and speed
- **Random events** test the margins the player left; reliability sets the odds

**Scoring (separate):** Goal ✔ · Science · Budget left · Safety margin.
**Debrief:** uses the player's own numbers, plus a "what real NASA did" fact.

### 6.4 Level goals (not solutions)
| Level | Required | Optional | New unlocks |
|---|---|---|---|
| L1 BEACON-1 | Orbit + transmit 30 days | Radiation data | 3 rockets, 3 orbits, battery/solar |
| L2 BISCUIT-1 | Orbit + return Biscuit safely | Film the flight | Heat shields, life support, parachutes |
| L3 SOLO | Jo: 3 orbits + safe return | Experiments | Crew systems, comms windows |
| L4 TANDEM | Two craft dock | Spacewalk | Maneuver fuel, data storage |
| L5 SURVEYOR-L | Soft-land probe on Moon | Landing-site photos | Distance, landing fuel, lunar night |
| L6 Project [NAME] | Crewed landing + return | Samples, experiments | Everything + Dad's partial plan as a starting point |

**L6 special mechanic:** Dad's sketches pre-fill a few parts (with his handwritten notes beside them). The player can keep, change or remove each one. No right answer. Characters react: Dot ("He'd have changed that too"), Jo ("Your call, Flight").

**Sandbox mode:** unlocked after L1.

## 7. Mission Outcome Cutscenes

Free design means outcomes aren't scripted, so cutscenes are tied to the **type of result** the sim produces, and they're emotional, not just technical. Each level has its own version with that level's characters.

### 7.1 Success cutscenes (per level)
| Level | Scene |
|---|---|
| L1 | Mission control erupts; Sparks hears the first beep and puts it on speaker. Protagonist calls home from a payphone. Mom doesn't answer. They leave a message: "...Mom. It worked." |
| L2 | Capsule splashes down. Biscuit is pulled out wagging. Dot cries into mug #3. Biscuit picks the protagonist and won't leave them. |
| L3 | Jo's voice crackles back after the blackout: "Still here, kid." The protagonist lets out a breath they've been holding. Pike claps once, quietly. |
| L4 | The two craft dock. Victor fist-bumps the protagonist without thinking, then pretends he didn't. |
| L5 | First photo from the Moon's surface comes in line by line on the CRT. The whole room goes silent. Fenwick: "Walter would've framed it." Protagonist pins it in Dad's office. |
| L6 | CS-10 Finale. |

### 7.2 Failure cutscenes (by failure type, reused across levels with level-specific lines)
Gentle, a little funny, and never mean. **No crew deaths:** crewed failures always end in an abort and safe rescue, so the stakes are emotional, not gory.

| Failure type | Scene | Emotional beat |
|---|---|---|
| **Launch blocked / rocket fails** | Rocket wobbles, a brick pops off, it plops into the ocean. Mission control groans. | Pike: "That was $12 million." Protagonist alone in Dad's office: "How did you do this every day?" |
| **Power runs out** | Satellite lights blink off one by one in orbit. Sparks keeps calling it, gets static. | Sparks takes off his headphones: "...We lost her." Quiet. |
| **Data/comms lost** | Mission technically works but nobody hears it. Fenwick stares at an empty printout. | Fenwick: "It's up there doing science and we'll never know what it found." |
| **Re-entry / landing fails (uncrewed)** | Capsule glows too hot, or the probe hits too hard. | Dot quietly: "Your dad lost one like this. He didn't sleep for a week." |
| **Crewed abort** (L3/L6) | Alarms, abort, capsule comes home early. Jo is safe but the mission's over. | Jo, at the hospital check-up: "Hey. I'm alive. That means you did your job." Protagonist breaks down a little. Jo hugs them. |
| **Over budget (not launchable)** | Pike rips up the request. | Pike: "No." But he lingers and adds: "...Try again. Cheaper." |

**After any failure:** A short debrief explaining exactly *why*, using the player's numbers. Then the player wakes up in Dad's office at night, the model rocket still broken, with a retry option. Failure never blocks the story.

**Partial success** (goal met, science or budget missed): Characters react mixed: "We did it... kind of." Small reward, still a promotion.

### 7.3 Quiet scenes (between levels, non-interactive or lightly interactive)
- Fixing a piece of the model rocket on Dad's desk after each success
- Mom's kitchen: one short scene per act, slowly warming up
- Walking Biscuit around the Center at night
- Victor and the protagonist eating vending-machine dinners

## 7.5 Money Struggles & Mid-Mission Events

### The Big Cut (story event, between L3 and L4, 1965)
- **What happens:** The war effort escalates. Congress moves money to the military, and the military gets priority on rocket fuel and propellant production. NASA's budget is cut, and **fuel becomes rationed**.
- **Cutscene "The Cut":** A CRT news report on military spending. Pike walks out of a meeting grey-faced: "They took 40%. And the fuel goes to the Air Force first." Dot drops a mug. Fenwick, quietly: "...So that's it?"
- **Gameplay change from L4 on:**
  - **Fuel cap:** each mission gets a max fuel allocation, not just a budget. You can't just strap on a bigger rocket.
  - **Fuel price spikes:** any fuel beyond the base ration costs triple.
  - The player is pushed toward lighter craft, smarter orbits, fewer redundant parts, and cutting science. Every trade-off gets sharper.
- **Emotional layer:** Victor's dad died in a war, and now war money is killing their program. Mom: "Your father fought this exact fight every year." Pike starts fighting *for* the protagonist for the first time.

### The Hearing (optional mini-scene, before L5)
- The protagonist has to defend the program to a Congressional committee (fictional senators).
- **Gameplay:** Senators ask pointed questions ("Why should taxpayers fund this over defense?"). The player answers by citing their *own* past mission results: cost per mission, science returned, success record.
- Good past performance (under budget, high science) → partial funding restored (bigger L5 budget). Poor record → stricter limits.
- So earlier choices carry forward. It's not a scripted punishment; it's your real track record.

### Random Mid-Level Events (during the design phase)
While the player is building, an event card can drop (1 per level, random). It changes the constraints mid-design and forces a rethink:

| Event | Effect | Who brings it |
|---|---|---|
| **Fuel shortage** | Fuel allocation −20% this mission | Pike |
| **Supplier strike** | One part type unavailable or double cost | Dot |
| **Budget freeze** | Budget −15% | Pike (via phone call from Washington) |
| **Part recall** | A part you picked has a defect: lower reliability unless replaced | Dot |
| **Bonus grant** | A university funds an extra instrument for free, if you can fit the mass | Fenwick |
| **Ground station down** | One comms station offline: fewer data windows | Sparks |
| **Launch delay / weather** | Orbit window shifts; mission lifetime shortened | Launch Office |
| **Military loan** | The Air Force offers a better rocket cheap, but it has priority and could be pulled last minute (risk!) | Pike |

- Events are announced with a short in-character scene (a phone ringing, someone bursting in).
- Some are good, some bad, some are gambles. Each one is a decision, not just a penalty.
- They never make a mission impossible; the sim always leaves at least one valid design.

## 9. Build Plan (2 days)

**Must-have demo (in order):**
1. Engine: campus hub + parts catalog + launch check + sim + debrief
2. Cutscene trigger system (enter-area / task-complete / sim-result)
3. Level 1 fully playable, plus its success and 1–2 failure cutscenes
4. Act I cutscenes: cold open, science fair flashback, funeral, first tape
5. Level 2 + Biscuit success scene
6. "To be continued" screen: the PROJECT [NAME] title page, then cut to black

**Stretch:** random event cards (easy: just modify limits), L3 + Jo scenes, Mom's kitchen scene, tape audio (record a teammate as Dad), sound effects, scoring stars.

**Pitch-only:** L4–6 as storyboards, the name reveal, the finale.

**Team split:**
- Coder(s): engine + levels (web/HTML-JS)
- Writer: dialogue, tapes, debrief text
- Artist: brick character sheet, campus, office, rocket parts
- Research/pitch: real 60s mission numbers, pitch video, AI-use disclosure

**Pitch line:** *"Mission design is all trade-offs. We made them personal: every choice is an argument between people you care about, and the last design you make is finishing a plan your father started for you."*
